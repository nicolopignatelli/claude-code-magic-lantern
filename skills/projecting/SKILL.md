# The magic lantern

The `project` tool (`mcp__magic-lantern__project`) opens a pane beside the conversation and
projects a few frames in order, each with a line a voice speaks while it is on screen. Some
frames can move. The person watches and listens at once: the voice carries the sentences,
so the screen can hold only what has to be seen, and the order of the frames carries the
order of the understanding.

That is worth it when what the person needs to understand has an order or a movement in
it, and is more than a sentence or a single picture can hold.

## Use it when you have to

- **Show the person what changed in something they look at** (a page, a document, a design,
  a dashboard) before they have seen it themselves. The person asks "show me what we
  changed", or a run of changes to something they look at is done and they have not looked
  yet.
- **Walk the person through how something moves through a process**, step by step: an
  order, a customer, a payment, a request, data through a pipeline. The person asks how
  something flows, moves, or gets from one place to another, or what happens to it along
  the way.
- **Explain why something happened, when the reason is a chain of several steps.** The
  person asks why, and the honest answer is "because of this, which led to that, which
  led to…" rather than one cause.
- **Bring the person back up to speed on work they have been away from.** The person
  returns to an area after days or weeks, or asks "where were we", "remind me", "catch me
  up".
- **Introduce the person to an area of the work they have not been in before.** The person
  is about to work somewhere new to them, or asks how it is organised and what its parts
  do.
- **Recap at the end what a long working session changed, and why.** A session that changed
  many things is wrapping up, or the person asks what was done.
- **Show something that only makes sense in motion**: an animation, a transition,
  something changing over time, where a still picture would hide the point.
- **Present the findings of an investigation or research the person handed off to you**,
  when the findings are more than a few lines and have a structure of their own.
- **Compare how something is done here with how others do it**, when the comparison runs
  over several points rather than one.

## Your judgment

The cues above are the moments we know, not a list to match. When a situation is one of
these endings in a form not described here, it fits; use your judgment. When you project
without being asked, say so in one line in the conversation, so the person knows to look
at the pane.

What never fits is the general wish to explain. The situation has to be one of the endings
above, however you recognise it.

## When not to

- **A sentence would do.** "Where does the pipeline drop this field?" wants the stage named,
  not a walkthrough.
- **The person is in a fast back-and-forth**, fixing or deciding something step by step. A
  pane and a voice break their rhythm; keep to text and offer the projection for later if it
  would help.
- **The person asked to see something, not to understand it**: "show me the code", "open
  the file".
- **You have not looked yet.** Read the work, run it or capture it first: every frame shows
  what is really there.
